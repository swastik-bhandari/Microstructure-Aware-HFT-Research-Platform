Interactive Replay

Watch the model predict, second by second

The CNN-LSTM issues a 30-second-ahead direction call each second. Play it back: the price line is colored by the model's prediction, and the running accuracy converges toward its true out-of-sample rate. Predictions are issued only after 60s of history — the model never sees the future.

The Directional Signal

A 54% predictor — and an honest null

The CNN-LSTM reads 60-second windows of 10 features and predicts whether price moves up or down over the next 30 seconds. It reaches 54.0% test accuracy — a real but modest edge over random. The harder question: does that signal actually improve execution?

CNN

Convolutions scan the window for local patterns — bursts of intensity, sharp imbalance swings — regardless of where they occur.

LSTM

Reads those patterns in temporal order across the 60-second window, compressing the sequence into one directional state.

P(up) → PPO

The output probability becomes an optional 7th feature for the execution agent — letting us measure its real contribution.

The null result: adding the signal changed execution by −0.12 ± 0.66 bps — statistically zero. A model can forecast direction at 54% and still not help execution. Direction prediction ≠ execution edge.

The Headline Finding

The apparent edge depends on the fill model

Under an idealized fill model, PPO appears to beat VWAP by ~19 bps. Swap in a real trade-attribution fill model — where a passive order only fills if a real buyer actually traded at its price — and on this data the gap narrows to a statistical tie (+0.08 bps). Why it matters: on this dataset, most of the agent's apparent advantage came from the fill model, not the policy. It is a single-day case study — not proof about RL execution in general — but it shows concretely that fill-model realism can dominate the measured result, and is worth reporting alongside any RL-vs-baseline comparison.

The Fairness Check
